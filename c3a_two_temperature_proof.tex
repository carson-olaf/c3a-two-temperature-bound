\subsection{Status and result}\label{status-and-result}

This note gives a proof, with a Lean-verified finite instance in §6,
that

\[
\boxed{C_{3a}>1.21418}.
\]

The construction is not a retuning of the earlier six-generator mask.
Its inner alphabet is the much smaller numerical-semigroup truncation

\[
M=\langle4,5\rangle\cap[0,80]
 =\{0,4,5,8,9,10\}\cup[12,80],
\]

and its main new ingredient is a \textbf{two-temperature entropy
theorem}: the difference family and the sum-family upper bound are
evaluated at separate dual parameters. The old one-temperature theorem
forced those two parameters to coincide.

At the exact rational parameters

\[
x=\frac{8149}{10000},\qquad
 y=\frac{42571}{50000},\qquad
 R=\frac{249293}{10000},
\]

the display-only fixed-rate value is

\[
1+\frac{\rho-\sigma}{\log R}
 =1.21418089609778549420005709249\ldots,
\]

while the entropy-matched limiting value is

\[
2-\frac{\sigma}{\rho}
 =1.21418118095053104745496921148\ldots.
\]

The conservative claim is only the strict rationally certified
inequality \(C_{3a}>1.21418\).

The numerical inputs are checked by two independently structured
programs. The strict finite bound is also proved end to end in Lean 4,
as described in §6--7. The general limiting theorem remains a written
argument. This package is not an externally refereed publication.

\subsection{1. The finite-set principle}\label{the-finite-set-principle}

We use the following lemma from K. Gyarmati, F. Hennecart, and I. Z.
Ruzsa, \emph{Sums and differences of finite sets}, Functiones et
Approximatio Commentarii Mathematici \textbf{37} (2007), 175-186,
Section 2, pp.~178-179, DOI 10.7169/facm/1229618749.

\textbf{GHR finite-set lemma.} Let \(U\subset\mathbb N_0\) be finite and
nonempty, with \(0\in U\). Put

\[
s=|U+U|,\qquad d=|U-U|,\qquad q_U=2\max U+1.
\]

If \(d<q_U\), then

\[
\theta(U)=1+\frac{\log(d/s)}{\log q_U}
\]

is an admissible exponent for the GHR problem. More precisely, for every
\(K>1\) there are arbitrarily large finite integer sets \(A,B\) such
that \(|A+B|\le K|A|\) and

\[
|A-B|\ge
\left(\frac{2(K-1)}{3K}\right)^{5/4}|A+B|^{\theta(U)}.
\]

Thus a single finite \(U\) satisfying the strict difference-set
condition supplies a lower bound for \(C_{3a}\).

\subsection{2. Universal cyclic
compression}\label{universal-cyclic-compression}

The outer compression step does not depend on the numerical semigroup.

\subsubsection{Lemma 2.1 (cyclic quotient with controlled
collisions)}\label{lemma-2.1-cyclic-quotient-with-controlled-collisions}

Let \(V\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and contain zero. Let
\(W\subset V-V\) have \(T=|W|\ge2\), and suppose every coordinate of
every word in \(W\) has absolute value at most \(B_0\). If \(p\) is
prime and

\[
p>2B_0,\qquad p>|V-V|,
\]

then there is a linear map

\[
\phi_c(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i z_i\pmod p
\]

such that, after multiplying \(\phi_c\) by a nonzero scalar if
necessary, the least-nonnegative representative set

\[
A=\operatorname{rep}_{[0,p-1]}\phi_c(V)
\]

satisfies

\[
\max A=p-1,
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
|A+A|\le2|V+V|,
\tag{2.2}
\]

\[
|A-A|>\frac{T}{2},
\tag{2.3}
\]

and

\[
|A-A|\le2|V-V|-1<2p-1=2\max A+1.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The coefficient vector can be chosen canonically by conditional
expectations, followed by canonical rescaling.

\subsubsection{Proof}\label{proof}

Choose all coefficients \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\) independently and uniformly
in \(\mathbb F_p\). For distinct \(w,w'\in W\), write \(h=w-w'\). Some
coordinate \(h_j\) is a nonzero integer with \(|h_j|\le2B_0<p\), and
hence is nonzero in \(\mathbb F_p\). Holding the other coefficients
fixed, exactly one value of \(c_j\) solves \(\phi_c(h)=0\). Therefore

\[
\Pr(\phi_c(w)=\phi_c(w'))=\frac1p.
\]

If \(C(c)\) is the number of unordered colliding pairs in \(W\), then

\[
\mathbb E C(c)=\frac{\binom T2}{p}<\frac T2,
\]

because \(T\le|V-V|<p\). Choose \(c\) with \(C(c)<T/2\). This choice can
be made coefficient by coefficient by minimizing the conditional
expected final collision count and breaking ties by the least residue.

If a fiber of \(\phi_c|_W\) has size \(t\), it loses \(t-1\) image
points but contributes \(\binom t2\ge t-1\) colliding pairs.
Consequently

\[
|\phi_c(W)|\ge T-C(c)>T/2.
\]

Since \(T\ge2\), the image \(\phi_c(V)\) is not \(\{0\}\). Choose the
least specified \(v\in V\) with \(\phi_c(v)\ne0\), and multiply the map
by \(-\phi_c(v)^{-1}\). This does not change any cardinality and puts
\(-1\) in the image. Since zero is also in the image, the representative
set contains \(0,p-1\), proving (2.1).

Every residue has at most two representatives in \([0,2p-2]\), so

\[
|A+A|\le2|\phi_c(V+V)|\le2|V+V|.
\]

Distinct residues in \(\phi_c(W)\) give distinct integer differences,
which proves (2.3). In \([-(p-1),p-1]\), residue zero has one
representative and each nonzero residue has at most two. Hence

\[
|A-A|\le2|\phi_c(V-V)|-1\le2|V-V|-1<2p-1.
\]

This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

\subsection{3. The two-temperature
theorem}\label{the-two-temperature-theorem}

Let \(M\subset\mathbb N_0\) be finite, contain zero, and contain a
positive integer. Define

\[
S=M+M,\qquad D=M-M,
\]

and, for \(d\in D\),

\[
\kappa(d)=\min\{a+b:a,b\in M,\ a-b=d\}.
\]

For \(x,y\in(0,1)\), put

\[
P_-(x)=\sum_{d\in D}x^{\kappa(d)},
\qquad
P_+(y)=\sum_{s\in S}y^s,
\]

\[
\mu=
\frac{\sum_{d\in D}\kappa(d)x^{\kappa(d)}}{P_-(x)},
\]

\[
\rho=\log P_-(x)-\mu\log x,
\qquad
\sigma=\log P_+(y)-\mu\log y.
\]

\subsubsection{Theorem 3.1 (two-temperature cyclic
quotient)}\label{theorem-3.1-two-temperature-cyclic-quotient}

If \(\sigma<\rho\), then

\[
\boxed{
C_{3a}\ge2-\frac{\sigma}{\rho}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

More generally, for every real \(r>\rho\),

\[
\boxed{
C_{3a}\ge1+\frac{\rho-\sigma}{r}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

In particular, if a rational number \(R>1\) satisfies \(\rho<\log R\),
then

\[
\boxed{
C_{3a}\ge1+\frac{\rho-\sigma}{\log R}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

\subsubsection{Proof}\label{proof-1}

Give \(D\) the symmetric Gibbs distribution

\[
p_d=\frac{x^{\kappa(d)}}{P_-(x)}.
\]

Its entropy is

\[
H(p)=-\sum_dp_d\log p_d
=\log P_-(x)-\mu\log x
=\rho.
\]

For every positive \(d\in D\), set

\[
n_d^{(n)}=n_{-d}^{(n)}=\lfloor np_d\rfloor,
\]

and put the unused count into zero:

\[
n_0^{(n)}=n-2\sum_{d>0}\lfloor np_d\rfloor.
\]

These counts are nonnegative, sum to \(n\), and their normalized
frequencies converge to \(p\). Moreover,

\[
\sum_d n_d^{(n)}\kappa(d)
=2\sum_{d>0}\lfloor np_d\rfloor\kappa(d)
\le n\mu.
\]

Let

\[
L_n=\left\lfloor\frac{n\mu}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]

\[
V_n=\left\{a\in M^n:\sum_i a_i\le L_n\right\},
\]

and let \(W_n\subset D^n\) be the type class with counts \(n_d^{(n)}\).

Choose minimum-cost witnesses \(\alpha_d,\beta_d\in M\) with

\[
\alpha_d-\beta_d=d,
\qquad
\alpha_d+\beta_d=\kappa(d),
\]

choosing the witnesses for \(-d\) by swapping those for \(d\). Every
word in \(W_n\) has coordinate sum zero. Thus its total
\(\alpha\)-budget equals its total \(\beta\)-budget, while their sum is
an even integer at most \(n\mu\). Each budget is therefore at most
\(L_n\), and

\[
W_n\subset V_n-V_n.
\]

Writing \(T_n=|W_n|\), Stirling's formula, or the standard
method-of-types bounds on a fixed alphabet, gives

\[
\frac1n\log T_n\longrightarrow H(p)=\rho.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Now use separate exponential tilts on the two sides. Every vector in
\(V_n+V_n\) has coordinates in \(S\) and total coordinate sum at most
\(2L_n\). Since \(0<y<1\),

\[
|V_n+V_n|y^{2L_n}\le P_+(y)^n.
\]

Because \(2L_n\le n\mu\),

\[
|V_n+V_n|\le e^{n\sigma}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Likewise, if \(z=a-b\in V_n-V_n\), then \(\kappa(z_i)\le a_i+b_i\)
coordinatewise. Hence

\[
|V_n-V_n|x^{2L_n}\le P_-(x)^n,
\]

and

\[
|V_n-V_n|\le e^{n\rho}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Fix \(r>\rho\). Put \(N_n=\lceil e^{rn}\rceil\), and use Bertrand's
postulate to choose a prime

\[
N_n<p_n<2N_n.
\]

For all sufficiently large \(n\), equations (3.4)-(3.6) give

\[
p_n>|V_n-V_n|,\qquad p_n>2\max M,\qquad T_n\ge2.
\]

Apply Lemma 2.1 to \(V_n,W_n,p_n\). It gives a finite
\(A_n\subset[0,p_n-1]\) satisfying the strict GHR hypothesis and

\[
|A_n-A_n|>T_n/2,
\qquad
|A_n+A_n|\le2e^{n\sigma},
\]

\[
2\max A_n+1=2p_n-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\theta(A_n)>
1+\frac{\log T_n-n\sigma-\log4}{\log(2p_n-1)}.
\]

By (3.4), the numerator after the initial 1, divided by \(n\), tends to
\(\rho-\sigma\). The denominator divided by \(n\) tends to \(r\). For
every number strictly below the right side of (3.2), one sufficiently
large fixed \(n\) therefore supplies a finite GHR seed. Applying the GHR
lemma to that single seed proves (3.2). Letting \(r\downarrow\rho\)
proves (3.1), and putting \(r=\log R\) gives (3.3). \(\square\)

\subsubsection{What is new relative to the one-temperature
theorem}\label{what-is-new-relative-to-the-one-temperature-theorem}

The earlier cyclic-quotient proof used \(y=x\), so its numerator
collapsed to \(\log P_-(x)-\log P_+(x)\). The sum upper bound does not
require this: its Markov parameter can be optimized independently. The
new theorem uses the difference tilt \(x\) to choose the high-entropy
realizable type and the sum tilt \(y\) only to upper-bound all possible
sums at the resulting budget.

For the gapless limiting alphabet \(\mathbb N_0\), taking \(x=\sqrt2-1\)
and \(y=1/2\) gives

\[
\rho=2\log(1+\sqrt2),
\qquad
\sigma=2\log2,
\]

and therefore

\[
2-\frac{\sigma}{\rho}
=2-\frac{\log2}{\log(1+\sqrt2)}
=1.213560298642605\ldots.
\]

Thus the independently announced \(1.21356\ldots\) result of Logan
Kleinwaks is the natural gapless baseline of the two-temperature
theorem. The construction below crosses it by changing the inner
alphabet, not by changing a cutoff in the old mask.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{4. The structured alphabet
\(\langle4,5\rangle\)}{4. The structured alphabet \textbackslash langle4,5\textbackslash rangle}}\label{the-structured-alphabet-langle45rangle}

Let

\[
H=\langle4,5\rangle.
\]

It has conductor \(12\) and exactly six gaps:

\[
\mathbb N_0\mathbin{\backslash} H=\{1,2,3,6,7,11\}.
\]

Take

\[
M=H\cap[0,80].
\]

Exact reconstruction gives

\[
|M|=75,
\qquad
|M+M|=155,
\qquad
|M-M|=161.
\]

In fact,

\[
M+M=\{0,4,5,8,9,10\}\cup[12,160],
\]

while

\[
M-M=[-80,80].
\]

For every positive \(d\in H\cap[1,80]\), the witness \((d,0)\) gives
\(\kappa(d)=d\). The six missing positive differences are recovered at
the following exact costs:

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
d&1&2&3&6&7&11\\ \hline
\kappa(d)&9&18&13&14&17&19.
\end{array}
\]

Thus the semigroup deletes six low-cost sum symbols but retains every
difference symbol, paying a moderate witness-cost penalty only on those
six gaps. That trade is exactly what improves the entropy ratio.

The partition polynomials can be written explicitly as

\[
P_+(y)=1+y^4+y^5+y^8+y^9+y^{10}+\sum_{s=12}^{160}y^s,
\]

and

\[
P_-(x)=1+2\left(
\sum_{d\in H\cap[1,80]}x^d
+x^9+x^{18}+x^{13}+x^{14}+x^{17}+x^{19}
\right).
\]

\subsection{6. The Lean-verified finite GHR
seed}\label{the-lean-verified-finite-ghr-seed}

The formal proof uses one finite depth

\[
N=10^{13},\qquad x=\frac{8149}{10000},\qquad y=\frac{42571}{50000}.
\]

Let \(D=M-M=[-80,80]\) and \(Z=P_-(x)\). For \(d>0\), set

\[
n_d=n_{-d}=\left\lfloor\frac{Nx^{\kappa(d)}}Z\right\rfloor,
\qquad
n_0=N-2\sum_{d=1}^{80}n_d,
\qquad
L=\sum_{d=1}^{80}n_d\kappa(d).
\]

The exact finite checks in
\texttt{formalization/lean/C3a/CertificateData.lean} prove that all

\[

\[
\mathcal V=\left\{a\in M^N:\sum_i a_i\le L\right\}.

\(W\subset\mathcal V-\mathcal V\), and exact finite type counting gives

\[
|W|\prod_{d\in D}n_d!=N!.
\]

These statements and the independent tilted upper bounds

\[
|\mathcal V+\mathcal V|y^{2L}\le P_+(y)^N,
\qquad
|\mathcal V-\mathcal V|x^{2L}\le Z^N

are proved in \texttt{C3a/VectorWords.lean}. No limit is taken.

The common envelope

covers the floors and the extra zero symbols. Effective Stirling bounds

The rational logarithm bounds in \texttt{C3a/LogNumerics.lean} establish
the following conservative integer bounds, assembled in
\texttt{C3a/Main.lean}:

\end{aligned}

postulate supplies a prime

\[

\]

In particular, \(p>160\) and \(p>|\mathcal V-\mathcal V|\). The finite
collision-counting proof in \texttt{C3a/CyclicCompression.lean},
together with \texttt{C3a/IntegerCompression.lean}, gives a linear map
\(\varphi:\mathbb Z^N\to\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) such that the

\]

Moreover, since \(|\mathcal V-\mathcal V|\ge1\),

\[
4\max A+1\le1296|\mathcal V-\mathcal V|,
\qquad
\log(4\max A+1)\le r+\log1296<r+8.

\tag{6.1}
\]

whose exact positive slack is \(36384716.28656\). Since \(\log4<2\),
this implies that the GHR exponent of \(V\) exceeds \(1+\alpha\). The
closed Lean theorem is

requires the construction for every \(K>1\), so its lower bound also
implies the bound under the definition in Tao's problem 3a.

The prime, coefficient vector, and finite sets are specified by
existence proofs, not materialized. The rate \(R=249293/10000\) belongs
to the limiting comparison in §5; it is not used to choose the prime in
this finite proof.
